My approach

Values

01
Connection

The focus is not only the “identified patient” but the whole family system. This is care built on lasting bonds, care that walks with people over time.

02
Autonomy

I care for people with severe, persistent conditions through the lens of recovery and citizenship. Autonomy means being able to steer one’s own life with dignity, because no person is reducible to a diagnosis.

03
Respect

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD), ADHD, and other neurodivergent profiles are variations in human development. Assessment is careful, language is respectful, and the plan has to make sense in real life.

04
Clarity

Every decision is anchored in evidence and communicated clearly, without jargon and without guesswork.

ESG, health, and citizenship

No one falls ill alone

I have learned, in the exam room and beyond it, that a person’s health is almost always the health of the systems they live in. I carry this conviction into the institutions where healthcare is organized at scale.

“I am because we are, and since we are, therefore I am.” John Mbiti, Kenyan philosopher and theologian Ubuntu comes from the Bantu worldviews of southern Africa, and it holds that a person does not exist alone, but within the fabric of their relationships with the community, with those who came before and with the land that sustains them. Each person’s health is inseparable from the health of that whole, and this is the understanding I deepened at Yale, where care is understood as citizenship and belonging.
“We are called to assist the Earth to heal her wounds and in the process heal our own, to embrace the whole creation in all its diversity, beauty and wonder.”
Wangari Maathai
A record of impact

What I hear in the consulting room does not fit inside it. I carry the ESG agenda, environmental, social and governance, into medical education, into the boards I sit on and into mental health policy, because Ubuntu says what the clinic confirms every day, that no one falls ill alone and no one heals alone. When a medical school changes the way it looks after its students, or a board decides where the money goes, the effect reaches people I will never see in person.
